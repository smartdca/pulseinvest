1. Services: the broker is Alpaca (a live account, priced in US dollars). The multiplier comes from DCA Score, queried live before every order. A scheduled job on Vercel runs once every US trading day. Order records are kept in a Google Sheet and compiled into this page’s data after each trading day.

2. How we invest: every asset follows the same rules, with no per-asset parameters. Each trading day, one market order per asset, placed by dollar amount (stocks are day orders; crypto orders stay open until cancelled). The base amount is $1 per asset per day, the broker’s minimum for small orders, so we use the smallest amount to observe what the DCA Score multiplier does. The size of the amount is not the point. Amount invested = base amount × DCA Score multiplier, which is never below 1.0: it only boosts, it never reduces.

3. How the numbers are calculated: invested is the amount actually filled, usually about 1% to 2% below the ordered amount. Value is each trading day’s closing price × total quantity held. Return = (value − invested) ÷ invested. Boost rate = orders with a multiplier above 1.0 ÷ all filled orders. Data updates once per trading day, so this page shows the previous trading day’s results.

4. Costs and notes: stocks are commission-free, but a tiny regulatory fee (about $0.01 per trading day) is added to the invested cost; crypto carries a trading fee, which is deducted from the quantity held. Both are already included in the figures shown. The account number, cash balance and order IDs are not published. This is our own test record, for reference only. It is not investment advice, and past results do not represent the future.

5. How the two are compared: same days, same assets, same daily prices. The DCA Score strategy line is the real account. The traditional DCA line is simulated: a fixed $1 per asset on each order day, with the same fee rules as above (crypto fee taken from the quantity held, US stock regulatory fee added to cost). XIRR turns the timing and size of every deposit into one annual rate, so different amounts and dates do not tilt the result. Annualizing a short period magnifies swings. The charts rescale as the numbers grow. The S&P 500 figure is a reference: the same total daily amount as the traditional group, invested in an S&P 500 ETF (SPY) on the same days, price only, before fees and dividends.

6. The numbers under “The experiment, in numbers”: Orders placed counts trading days when every order filled; misses are reported as they happen. Boosted orders are orders above the base amount. Biggest boost is the largest single-day multiplier. The gap is the strategy minus traditional DCA, in percentage points, and “this week” means the last five trading days. Lower average cost counts the assets whose average buy price, fees included, is below traditional DCA’s. Versus the S&P 500 is the strategy’s return minus the S&P 500 reference, in percentage points. Weekly log: each tile is one week, colored by how much the strategy’s return changed that week (light under 1%, medium 1% to 3%, dark 3% or more).

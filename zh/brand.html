The long version

Everyone wants to get better at investing.

When the market drops, we tell ourselves we’ll buy the dip — just not yet. Then it drops further, and we start hesitating. By the time it starts bouncing back, we’re watching the chart climb without us. Eventually we give in and buy… only to watch it drop again. When prices are rising, we’re scared of buying too high. When they’re falling, we wait for an even better price. And sometimes we think we’ve sold at the perfect moment, only to realize we got out too early.

The longer you invest, the more homework it feels like there is.

Prices. Charts. News. Earnings. Expert opinions. There’s always one more thing to check. But more information doesn’t always make the decision easier. When we want to buy, we look for confirmation. When we see others making money, FOMO kicks in and we chase the market. Before long, a carefully thought-out plan turns into a decision made on instinct. The hard part isn’t finding a strategy. It’s sticking to it when it’s time to act.

Traditional DCA only solves half the problem.

Invest on a schedule. Invest a fixed amount. Keep going whether the market feels hot or scary. It takes the “should I invest today?” question off the table. But when it’s time to actually place the order, hesitation can still creep in. The price is moving. Maybe it’s too expensive. Maybe it’s better to wait. And just like that, the plan gets adjusted — one decision at a time.

One last look. 24/7.

You don’t need to guess whether tomorrow will be up or down, and you don’t need to call the bottom. DCA Score simply turns the real-time state of an asset into a score. That score decides how much goes in this time — the normal amount, or more, never less. A final check after the research, right before the order goes in. The schedule stays the same. The score just adjusts the size.

We don’t tell you what to buy. We don’t predict where the market is going. And we don’t promise returns. We just believe in three things: discipline, conviction, and actually doing it. Follow the rules, trust the method instead of the forecast, and then pull the trigger. That’s why we put real money into a public record and follow the same rules for the long run.

You don’t need to get every market move right. You need a method you can actually stick with.
